  • About the author

A Head of Growth told us their page-one Google rankings were holding, traffic was fine, and yet buyers kept saying they had "asked ChatGPT" and never heard the brand's name. The first question in that audit was not about keywords. It was simpler: can the machine even read the page?

This guide explains what an AI crawlability check tests, why JavaScript breaks it, and what to do once your pages are readable.

Chapter 01/ 091 min

Why is AI crawlability the first step in GEO?

The short answer

Because an AI engine cannot cite a page it cannot read. Most AI crawlers fetch your raw HTML and do not run JavaScript, so content that only appears after scripts load can look like an empty page to them. Crawlability is the gate before every other GEO tactic.

Search is splitting into two games. One is ranking a link. The other is being the answer an AI engine gives a buyer. In that second game there is no page two: an answer names a handful of brands, and if you are not one of them you are not in the conversation.

You can have the best answer in your category and still lose, simply because the engine never read it. That is why we treat crawlability as table stakes for GEO, not a technical afterthought in an engineering backlog.

Chapter 02/ 091 min

What does an AI crawlability checker test?

The short answer

It tests whether crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot can reach your page and read its content. The key question is whether your important content is in the HTML the server sends, or only appears after JavaScript runs in a browser.

Raw HTML vs the rendered page

Every page exists in two states. The raw HTML is the file your server sends before anything runs. The rendered page is what a person sees after JavaScript has built the rest. People always see the rendered page. Most AI crawlers stop at the raw file.

A checker makes that gap visible before it costs you: it shows what a crawler gets, so you can see what is missing.

i

TipReadable is the floor, not the finish.

Passing a crawlability check does not mean you will be cited. It means you are eligible to be considered. The rest of this guide covers both halves.

Chapter 03/ 091 min

Which AI crawlers can reach and read your site?

The short answer

Two gates decide it. First access: your robots.txt must allow each crawler. Then rendering: even when allowed, the main AI crawlers read HTML without running JavaScript. Google's crawler is the exception, and it feeds Gemini and AI Overviews.

AI crawler access and rendering by platform
CrawlerUsed byRuns JavaScript?
GPTBot, OAI-SearchBotOpenAI · ChatGPT and ChatGPT searchNo
ClaudeBotAnthropic · ClaudeNo
PerplexityBotPerplexityNo
GooglebotGoogle Search, Gemini, AI OverviewsYes
ApplebotAppleYes

Crawler names matter. OpenAI, for example, runs separate agents for training, for search and for fetching pages a user asks about. Blocking the wrong one by accident can remove you from ChatGPT search. Check each name against the engine's own documentation before you write a rule.

Chapter 04/ 091 min

How do you check what an AI crawler sees on your page?

The short answer

Turn JavaScript off in your browser and reload the page: what stays is roughly what GPTBot or ClaudeBot reads. You can also view the page source and look for your body copy, or run the page through the checker above.

  1. Disable JavaScript and reload. Open your browser settings, turn JavaScript off and reload a key page. Note what disappears.
  2. View the source. Open the raw page source and search for a sentence from your main content, your pricing and your FAQ.
  3. Check robots.txt. Open /robots.txt and confirm the AI crawlers you want are not blocked.
  4. Run the checker. Use the tool at the top of this page for a score and a list of fixes.
Chapter 05/ 091 min

Which rendering setups make pages invisible to AI?

The short answer

Pure client-side rendering is the main cause: single-page apps send an almost empty HTML shell and build the page with JavaScript. Hybrid setups are riskier than they look, because reviews, pricing or FAQs often load late and stay invisible.

The fix is to render important content before it reaches the browser. Match the method to the page:

Choosing a rendering method
MethodHow it worksBest for
SSR · server-side renderingThe server builds the full HTML on each requestPages that change often
SSG · static generationPages are built ahead of time, at deployStable pages: comparisons, product and pricing pages
ISR · incremental regenerationStatic pages that rebuild on a scheduleLarge catalogs that update now and then
!

Watch outThe hybrid trap.

Your main copy may render on the server and look fine, while the blocks buyers ask about most (reviews, pricing, FAQs) load later with JavaScript and never reach the crawler. Check those blocks specifically.

Chapter 06/ 091 min

Does site structure affect AI crawlability too?

The short answer

Yes. A readable page still has to be found. If deep pages are only reachable through one hub, crawlers can miss them. Link related pages to each other directly, and keep key content such as a help center inside your main site where you can.

Think of an airline that routes every flight through one hub. Miss the hub and you reach nothing. A site that links related pages point to point gives crawlers many ways in, so nothing is stranded. We build every pillar this way: each spoke page sits one link from its hub.

Chapter 07/ 091 min

Will llms.txt, schema or page speed fix AI crawlability?

The short answer

No single file fixes it. llms.txt is an optional hint and cannot make JavaScript-only content readable. Schema describes content that is already in the HTML. Speed helps people more than citations. The real fix is putting important content in the raw HTML.

What moves the needle

Do

  • Put key content in the raw HTML with SSR or static generation
  • Allow the AI search crawlers you want in robots.txt
  • Link related pages to each other directly
  • Add schema that matches content already on the page

Don't

  • Expect llms.txt to fix content crawlers cannot read
  • Mark up content that only loads with JavaScript
  • Block unknown bots by default without checking which
  • Chase speed scores while key copy is missing from the HTML

If you still want to try llms.txt, our llms.txt generator makes it a five-minute test rather than a project.

Chapter 08/ 091 min

Once a page is readable, how do you get it cited?

The short answer

Readable makes a page eligible; trust gets it cited. Lead with direct answers, show real expertise and primary sources, and earn consistent mentions where buyers and models look. Start with the pages that drive revenue, not the whole site at once.

You do not need to re-render your entire site. Find the pages where buyers decide (product, comparison and pricing pages) and make those readable and citable first. The long tail can wait.

Then build the trust layer: answer-first structure, named authors, sources and mentions on independent sites. That is the heart of our AEO and GEO work.

Questions/ 091 min

Frequently asked questions

Is the AI crawlability checker free?

Yes. It is free to use and needs no sign-up or account.

What does the checker look at?

It fetches the page you enter and scores how easy it is for AI crawlers to reach and read, then lists recommendations to fix. Run it on your most important pages first.

If my page passes, will ChatGPT cite it?

Not automatically. Passing means the page is readable, so it can be considered. Being cited depends on how clearly it answers the question and how much engines trust the source.

Does blocking GPTBot remove me from ChatGPT?

Not necessarily. OpenAI documents separate crawlers for model training and for search. Blocking the training crawler is different from blocking the search crawler. Check each crawler's documentation before changing robots.txt.

How often should I run the check?

After any redesign, framework change or new page template, and on your key pages every few months. Rendering problems often arrive quietly with a site update.
